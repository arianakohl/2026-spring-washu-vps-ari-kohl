I would say I have a few practices in my life that are important for my psychological wellbeing and fulfillment. Number one is definitely spending quality time with my dog and my loved ones. It is difficult when I can’t be with them in person at school so carving out moments most every day to see them and interact with them on facetime and through receiving videos of my doggy are rituals that help me feel presently connected to my loved ones even when I can’t be in the same physical space with them for portions out of the year while I am at school. Love and connection are the most valuable things to me to pursue, maintain, and appreciate in this life so it makes sense that this practice brings me increased psychological fulfillment and wellbeing. Another thing is definitely organization. This one is tricky for me because I actually struggle a lot with staying organized, like keeping my room clean and my stuff in places I remember putting them in. It is something that often feels like a mounting task for me when it seemingly comes easily to a lot of other people. I definitely feel a lot more psychologically collected and centered when my laundry is folded and my bed is made (to name a couple) but it is difficult for me to maintain this without it becoming this overwhelming to-do list. I include this example because it is a practice/routine that I am currently striving in my life to improve at and find ways for it to not feel like a daunting addition to my days because it is something that I recognize does really improve my psychological state when I can surmount that mental block. This teaches me about the nature of human fulfillment, that sometimes things can be incredibly fulfilling and integral ingredients in your psychological wellbeing/fulfillment even when they aren’t things that originally come naturally to you, which I find to be an interesting dichotomy in human nature.
